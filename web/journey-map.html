Work streams: how photo data is captured on the day Later sprints

The journey above is the same for every job. What changes is stage 3, how each subject is tied to their photos. All jobs fall into three categories, which can be built as separate work streams on the existing architecture in later sprints.

ATablet QR

How it works
The client supplies a digital name list. The photographer loads it onto the tablet. At the shoot the subject gives a student ID or last name, the tablet generates a QR code, and the photographer scans it before the picture so the subject's data is attached to their photos.
Jobs
Sports and School portraits. QR codes are becoming more common in School jobs.
Who is involved
Photographer.
How the app responds
QR card on Assignment Details, shown beyond Sports wherever a list exists.

BPrinted QR card

How it works
B1 · Yearbook portraits. At schools still on the old system, each student gets a small paper card with their QR code while waiting in line. The photographer scans it before the picture. VIP distributes the cards on the day and hires a Check-in person to run it.
B2 · Blank yellow card (School and Sports). When a subject's data never reached the system and there is no QR code, the photographer hands over a blank yellow card with a new, unique QR code. The subject fills in their details. The photographer scans the card's QR code with the camera, then photographs the card, so the lab can process both together.
Who is involved
Photographer, athlete or student. For B1, a VIP-hired Check-in person manages the cards; this person does not use the app and the role is expected to be retired.
How the app responds
Capture-method card on Details (the Check-in person noted as text only) and a reminder about the yellow-card fallback and its scan-then-photograph order.

CManual group log

Today
C1 · Sports. Each group's starting and ending camera frame numbers are logged on paper, then typed in with the group name during upload. The client then names each athlete by looking up each group on their portal.
C2 · Service Photos (school clubs). The lead photographer always logs each group in a writeable PDF and uses a cheat sheet showing the arrangement of people for the number of people in each group. A school helper lines people up tallest to shortest as they are called to the stage. Afterwards the client verifies who is in each club on the portal.
Who is involved
Photographer or lead, school helper (C2), client.
How the app responds
Enter groups and frame ranges in the app, select and reorder groups as the day's schedule changes (C1). Digital group log and built-in arrangement cheat sheet for the lead (C2).

A parallel journey: managing time off Phase 2

Time off was the last part of "Get paid". It is an availability experience with its own trigger, goal and outcome, so it now runs alongside the assignment journey and meets it at the Schedule. PRD 8.2, 8.13

ANotice a conflict

Doing
A personal commitment appears against the coming weeks.
Pain today
Time off is handled informally, so conflicts surface late.
App responds
The Schedule shows assignments and time off in one app.

BRequest time off

Doing
Picks dates in the next 14 days; for a single date chooses All Day, Before Noon or Afternoon.
Pain today
Requests are made by email. Schedules are built a week or two ahead, so a request can be hard for the scheduler to fit in.
App responds
Time Off tab with a Request Time Off sheet.

CWait for a decision

Doing
The request stays Pending; it can be withdrawn.
Pain today
Confirmation also comes back by email, which is frustrating.
App responds
Request and decision stay in the app, with status grouped Pending, Approved, Denied, Cancelled.

Doing
Sees approved time off on their schedule.
Pain today
None noted. The schedule, including time off, already shows on a responsive website and through a data connection to Google Calendar.
App responds
Approved entries appear on the Schedule, with a detail screen, so requests and schedule live in one app.

EChange of plans

Emotion curve

One consistent scale from negative to positive across the seven stages. The two lowest points come after the shoot.

Illustrative, not validated research data. Potential pattern to validate with photographers.

Low · Stage 5 Upload photos

Hypothesis: tired and frustrated

Why it may happen
Backing up to a personal computer on a separate, unintuitive website is extra work, especially at the end of the day when the photographer is tired.
Design response
One place to upload from the memory card, with clear progress indication at any time after completing the shoot.

Low · Stage 6 Complete timesheet

Hypothesis: annoyed, worried about disputes

Why it may happen
Travel time, miles and tolls must be tracked separately and are usually recalled at the end of the day.
Design response
Time and distance captured as they happen and pre-filled; tolls and receipts added in one sheet; submit without waiting for upload.

High · Stage 3 Prepare and shoot

Hypothesis: focused and confident

Why it may happen
It is the work photographers came to do.
Design response
Keep one-tap calling and Report issue within reach, with nothing else competing for attention.

Takeaways

  1. The lowest points come after the shoot, which is why upload and timesheet are prioritized, and why they are independent of each other.
  2. Problems need a fast path, so the app gives one-tap contact and logs incidents.
  3. Trust in pay is the central theme: reliable records, clear rates and a visible breakdown.
  4. However subjects are identified (tablet QR, printed card or manual log), the photographer should record it once, in the field, so nothing is re-keyed at upload.

Empathy map: field photographer

Does

  • Checks the schedule and confirms details
  • Travels and logs start and arrival
  • Shoots, often alone
  • Uploads photos from a memory card
  • Fills in a timesheet with times, miles and tolls
  • Calls the scheduler when something goes wrong
  • Scans each subject's QR code before the picture, from the tablet or a printed card
  • Logs groups by camera frame numbers on paper when there is no QR code

Gains

  • Time and costs captured as they happen

  • Fewer timesheet disputes and missed assignments

What changed from version 1

  • Six stages became seven; "Deliver photos" is now "Upload photos", and upload and timesheet run in parallel after a new End Assignment stage.
  • Time off moved out of "Get paid" into its own parallel journey.
  • The emotion curve is now plotted on one scale, marked illustrative, with low points explained.
  • Empathy map: Says keeps its "no quotes yet" note, now with a labelled research-questions list, and a Hears section is new.
  • New section: three photo-data capture categories (tablet QR, printed card, manual group log) as separate work streams for later sprints.
  • Everything else (persona, method note, pain points, app responses, takeaways, Pains and Gains) is carried over.
